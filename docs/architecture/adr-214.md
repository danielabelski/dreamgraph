# ADR-214: Standalone Architect Adaptive Future Engine review records stay advisory, provenance-rich, and supersession-aware

**Date:** 2026-05-27T20:15:16.740Z  
**Status:** accepted  
**Decided by:** collaborative  
**Affected entities:** Adaptive Future Engine, standalone-architect, candidate futures, future-fit score, future objections, future review decisions, future supersession status, model provenance, fallback reason, ADR-203, ADR-204  

## Context

The migration plan required a durable review schema for plan-bound Adaptive Future Engine outputs. Without an explicit ADR, candidate futures, review decisions, provenance, and supersession handling could be inconsistently modeled or mistaken for hard enforcement.

**Constraints:**
- ADR-204 makes the Adaptive Future Engine advisory and explicitly forbids hard enforcement without new ADR review.
- ADR-203 model-routing order and fallback visibility remain binding for LLM-enabled AFE work.
- The plan already requires AFE plan surfaces to show candidate futures, future-fit score and evidence, future objections, prior outcomes, model provenance/fallback reason, human review decisions, and supersession/review status.
- Plan-bound future records need to support historical review and active decisioning without polluting durable project truth as if speculative futures were accepted architecture.

## Decision

Lock AFE plan review records to a provenance-rich, advisory schema that at minimum captures: candidate future identity, future-fit score and evidence, future objections, known prior future outcomes, model provenance, fallback reason when applicable, human review decision, review timestamp/actor context, and supersession status or replacement linkage. These records inform planning and review, but they do not become hard constraints or durable accepted architecture unless separately crystallized into ADRs or other durable project entities after review.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Treat AFE output as an implicit ranking with no durable review record** — Rejected: This loses provenance, decision history, and supersession context for plan-bound futures.
- **Promote winning futures directly into hard enforcement or accepted truth without review** — Rejected: ADR-204 forbids turning AFE into a hard enforcement mechanism without separate governance.
- **Store only a free-text note about future recommendations** — Rejected: That would make provenance, decision status, and supersession semantics too ambiguous for operational use.

## Consequences

- Standalone Architect can render explainable future-review UI without confusing advisory rankings for accepted truth.
- Future records can be compared, superseded, and resumed across plan work with clear provenance.
- AFE implementation remains aligned with advisory governance instead of drifting toward hidden enforcement.

**Risks accepted:**
- The initial schema may need additive fields as real review workflows expose missing nuance.
- Users may still over-trust scored futures unless the UI clearly distinguishes advisory signals from accepted decisions.

## Guard Rails

> ⚠️ Do not treat AFE candidate futures or scores as hard blockers or accepted architecture without separate reviewed crystallization.

> ⚠️ Do not omit model provenance, fallback reason, or supersession state from durable future-review records.

> ⚠️ Do not let AFE review UX hide future objections or prior outcome evidence when available.

> ⚠️ Do not collapse human review decisions into opaque score-only outcomes.
