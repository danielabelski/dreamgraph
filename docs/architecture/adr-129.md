# ADR-129: Adaptive Result Delivery Uses Targets, Expectations, and Explicit Compromise Instead of Hard Limits

**Date:** 2026-05-07T17:35:42.830Z  
**Status:** accepted  
**Decided by:** collaborative  
**Affected entities:** read_source_code, query_api_surface, DreamGraph tool transport, response serialization, runtime diagnostics  

## Context

DreamGraph tool and transport responses can vary in size and fidelity across producer, serialization, transport, client, and history layers. Modeling these behaviors as fixed hard result limits leads to incorrect reasoning, misleading diagnostics, and brittle implementation choices. For this kind of cognitive system, hard-size enforcement is a poor fit because it hides partial success and prevents adaptive recovery strategies.

**Constraints:**
- The system operates across multiple layers where payload degradation may occur after successful tool execution.
- Response sizing behavior is soft and context-dependent rather than governed by a single authoritative constant.
- The system must support progressive narrowing and recovery when large responses are difficult to deliver intact.
- Existing tooling and callers may need backward-compatible response evolution rather than abrupt clipping or hard failure semantics.

## Decision

DreamGraph will not model response delivery through fixed hard result limits as the primary policy. Instead, it will use adaptive targets and expectations for result delivery, and when ideal delivery cannot be preserved it should favor explicit compromise signaling and recovery guidance over hard-cap language or premature clipping.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Keep or introduce fixed hard result limits** — Rejected: This creates false certainty, encourages brittle behavior, conflates producer success with downstream delivery loss, and is a poor fit for adaptive cognitive tooling.
- **Silently clip oversized results without signaling** — Rejected: This destroys context, obscures failure modes, and prevents callers from recovering intelligently.
- **Treat truncation as purely a client concern with no architectural guidance** — Rejected: This leaves the system without a shared reasoning model and leads to inconsistent diagnostics and response-shaping decisions.

## Consequences

- Diagnostics will shift from hard-cap language toward target/expectation/compromise language.
- Tool and transport behavior can evolve adaptively without violating an artificial constant-limit model.
- Large-response handling should increasingly provide recovery hints and navigable structure rather than relying on clipping.
- Agents and humans will be better able to distinguish producer success from downstream compromise.

**Risks accepted:**
- Without careful wording, adaptive behavior may become too vague for implementers.
- Different subsystems may interpret targets and compromises inconsistently.
- Some callers may still assume hard ceilings and require migration in language or expectations.

## Guard Rails

> ⚠️ Do not describe result delivery as governed by a single hard constant unless code and runtime policy explicitly prove that is true.

> ⚠️ Prefer additive response metadata and explicit compromise signaling over silent clipping.

> ⚠️ When large payload delivery degrades, preserve or expose recovery paths such as narrowing, sub-entity targeting, or structured summaries.

> ⚠️ Distinguish tool execution success from downstream delivery fidelity in diagnostics and user-facing reasoning.
