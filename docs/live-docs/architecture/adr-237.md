# ADR-237: Committed graph revisions are isolated by an instance read/write reconciliation barrier

**Date:** 2026-08-14T11:14:01.695Z  
**Status:** accepted  
**Decided by:** collaborative  
**Affected entities:** feature_incremental_additive_scan_plan, data_scan_state_v1, data_store, graph_snapshot, cognitive_graph_rag, graph_enrichment  

## Context

ADR-236's journaled rollback prevents durable partial commits but explicitly permits unbarriered readers to observe a mixed set while files are replaced. Slice 4 requires every externally observable graph read to see exactly one complete committed reconciliation revision.

**Constraints:**
- Existing independent JSON stores and public JSON formats remain compatible
- Readers must observe one committed graph revision, never a mixed old/new set
- Full scan remains recovery authority
- Critical-section performance is secondary to correctness for v1
- Incremental authoritative reconciliation remains disabled until later slices

## Decision

Supersede ADR-236's reader-compatibility exception with a durable instance-level consistency policy: preview and staging remain outside the critical section; authoritative reconciliation acquires one exclusive graph barrier, revalidates the expected revision, journals prior and next bodies, replaces canonical stores, publishes scan_state.json last, invalidates caches, and releases only after success or complete rollback. Canonical multi-store readers hold the shared side for their entire snapshot. Ordinary enrichment, dream, scheduler, Architect/MCP, and manual atomic graph mutations use the exclusive side and cannot cross reconciliation.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Immutable per-revision snapshot directories plus active pointer** — Rejected: It would require a broader migration of all path consumers and persistence writers than Slice 4 needs; the centralized barrier proves the invariant without changing JSON locations.
- **ADR-236 journal-only commit with scan_state publication last** — Rejected: Rollback repairs disk but cannot retract a mixed snapshot already observed by an unbarriered reader.
- **Ad-hoc scan locks at individual reader and mutation call sites** — Rejected: Coverage would be fragile and legacy readers could bypass the contract; the common cache, snapshot, atomic-write, and reconciliation boundaries are narrower and coherent.

## Consequences

- Readers begun before commit finish on the prior complete revision
- Readers arriving during commit wait and then see the new complete revision or restored prior revision
- Canonical atomic mutations serialize against reconciliation
- Interrupted journals roll back during recovery; rollback failure remains explicitly recovery_required
- scan_state.json is the final publication write and caches are invalidated before readers resume

**Risks accepted:**
- Long multi-store reads can delay reconciliation and long commits can briefly delay reads
- Direct legacy filesystem readers outside canonical graph APIs require explicit audit/migration before they may claim multi-store consistency
- The barrier is daemon-process scoped; multiple independent daemon processes for one instance remain prohibited by the existing single-daemon ownership contract

## Guard Rails

> ⚠️ Do NOT expose a canonical multi-store read without holding the shared graph barrier for the full snapshot.

> ⚠️ Do NOT mutate canonical graph JSON outside the common atomic-write/mutation boundary.

> ⚠️ Do NOT release the exclusive reconciliation barrier before cache invalidation and complete success or rollback.

> ⚠️ Do NOT publish scan_state.json before every other transaction store replacement succeeds.

> ⚠️ Do NOT clear a journal after rollback failure; preserve recovery_required state.

> ⚠️ Do NOT enable authoritative incremental reconciliation until Slice 4 verification remains green.
