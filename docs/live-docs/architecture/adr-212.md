# ADR-212: Standalone Architect mutation review is a curated audited Keep-Undo review workflow

**Date:** 2026-05-27T20:14:46.737Z  
**Status:** accepted  
**Decided by:** collaborative  
**Affected entities:** standalone-architect, pending diffs, review gates, governed mutation endpoints, audit records, Keep action, Undo action, ADR guard-check warnings, ADR-087, ADR-144, ADR-206  

## Context

The migration plan required an exact mutation review contract for daemon-produced patches reviewed from the browser. Without an explicit decision, browser mutation flows could blur together direct edits, opaque approvals, or unaudited reverts.

**Constraints:**
- Browser clients do not gain direct filesystem authority; daemon-mediated mutation tools and governed intents remain authoritative per ADR-206 and ADR-144.
- ADR-087 requires curated mutation flows, audit context, and concurrency visibility rather than arbitrary writes.
- The UI needs explicit review actions for daemon-produced diffs and must surface ADR guard-check warnings before governed mutation.
- Fallbacks to non-MCP or non-preferred paths must remain visible and justified.

## Decision

Lock standalone Architect mutation review to a curated diff workflow where the daemon proposes or stages changes, the browser renders pending diffs and guard-rail warnings, and the user-facing review actions remain explicit and auditable. `Keep` means accept and retain the governed daemon-produced mutation outcome. `Undo` means discard or revert the pending outcome through daemon-governed reversal semantics rather than direct browser file writes. Review decisions, reasons, and guard-check visibility are part of the durable audit trail.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Let the browser directly edit files and save accepted patches** — Rejected: This bypasses daemon governance, auditability, and the established mutation authority model.
- **Auto-apply daemon-produced patches without an explicit review contract** — Rejected: This hides risk and weakens the user's ability to inspect or reject mutations before they land.
- **Treat Keep and Undo as informal UI labels without durable audit semantics** — Rejected: That would make review outcomes ambiguous and hard to reason about during resume, rollback, and provenance analysis.

## Consequences

- Phase 5 can implement a safe browser review loop without inventing ad hoc mutation semantics.
- Resume behavior can explain which diffs are pending, accepted, or undone with durable audit evidence.
- Review UX stays compatible with ADR guard-check surfacing and tool-tier fallback visibility.

**Risks accepted:**
- Exact revert mechanics may still need implementation detail work for multi-file or partially accepted changes.
- Users may expect broader git-style history operations than the initial governed Keep/Undo scope supports.

## Guard Rails

> ⚠️ Do not let the browser accept, edit, or revert code changes through direct filesystem authority.

> ⚠️ Do not hide ADR guard-check warnings or audit reasons when presenting daemon-produced mutations for review.

> ⚠️ Do not implement silent mutation application or silent revert behavior for standalone Architect review flows.

> ⚠️ Do not normalize non-MCP fallback mutation paths as routine; they must remain visible and justified.
